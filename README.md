# Nigeria DHS Immunization Analysis

Analysis of zero-dose and immunization dropout prevalence in Nigeria using Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) data from 2013, 2018, and 2024 to assess immunization system performance, equity gaps, and vaccination coverage trends.

## Overview

This repository presents a national analysis of zero-dose and immunization dropout prevalence among children aged 12–23 months in Nigeria using three rounds of the Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS).

The study examines trends in immunization access and continuity, using zero-dose prevalence and dropout prevalence as complementary indicators of immunization system performance. Findings provide evidence to support immunization planning, health systems strengthening, and equitable vaccine delivery strategies.

## Research Objectives

* Estimate national zero-dose prevalence across NDHS survey rounds.
* Assess immunization dropout prevalence and trends over time.
* Examine changes in immunization system performance between 2013 and 2024.
* Compare zero-dose and dropout indicators as complementary measures of immunization programme effectiveness.
* Generate evidence to support policy and programmatic decision-making.

## Data Source

* Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) 2013

## Methods

* Secondary analysis of nationally representative DHS datasets
* Data cleaning and harmonization across survey rounds
* Survey-weighted descriptive analysis
* Trend analysis
* Comparative analysis of immunization indicators
* Data visualization and interpretation using R

## Key Indicators

### Zero-Dose Prevalence

Children aged 12–23 months who had not received any routine childhood vaccination.

### Immunization Dropout Prevalence

Children who initiated vaccination but failed to complete the recommended vaccination schedule.

## Key Findings

* Zero-dose prevalence remains a major challenge in Nigeria despite improvements in immunization services.
* Dropout prevalence provides complementary information on continuity of care and programme performance.
* Trends reveal important opportunities for targeted interventions and health systems strengthening.
* Findings highlight the need for equitable strategies that improve both vaccine access and completion.
